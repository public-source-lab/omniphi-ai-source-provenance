# Research Methodology

## Purpose

This repository documents public-source due diligence and source-provenance research concerning OmniPhi (omniphi.ai) / Algoriphi, Inc.

The purpose of this repository is not to establish in advance that OmniPhi is legitimate or illegitimate. The objective is to document what can and cannot be independently verified from publicly available evidence.

## Evidence and Source Provenance

Search-result volume is not treated as evidence of independent corroboration.

Public material is classified according to its origin and relationship to other sources. Where multiple websites reproduce substantially identical material originating from the same press release, company statement, wire distribution, or other originating source, those publications are treated as derivative or syndicated sources rather than independent confirmation.

The investigation therefore attempts to identify the originating source before assessing the number of apparently separate publications supporting a claim.

## Source Categories

### Primary / High-Value Evidence

Examples include:

- Government and regulatory records
- Court decisions and filings
- Corporate registry records
- Securities-exchange records
- Official company filings
- Independent security audits
- Independently verifiable technical documentation
- Independently verified trading or financial records
- Directly attributable statements from relevant parties

### Company-Controlled or Company-Distributed Material

Examples include:

- Company websites
- Company-authored documentation
- Company press releases
- Press-release distribution services
- Company-controlled social-media accounts
- Promotional material distributed on behalf of the company

These sources can establish what a company claims, but do not by themselves establish that the underlying claims are independently verified.

### Derivative / Syndicated Material

This includes material that substantially reproduces information originating from another source, including:

- Press-release syndication
- Reprints
- Translations of the same promotional material
- Articles using substantially identical campaign copy
- Aggregator pages
- Affiliate or partner publications reproducing supplied material

Derivative sources are documented where relevant but are not counted as independent corroboration of the underlying claim.

### Promotional / Influencer Material

Social-media posts, influencer content, affiliate material, sponsored content, and partner publications are treated as promotional evidence where the available evidence indicates a commercial or promotional relationship.

The existence of a promotional relationship does not by itself establish misconduct.

## Claims vs. Findings

The repository distinguishes between:

**Company claim**  
A statement made by OmniPhi, Algoriphi, its representatives, or promotional partners.

**Independent finding**  
A fact supported by evidence that can be independently examined.

**Partially verified**  
A claim for which some underlying facts can be independently established but the full claim cannot.

**Open question**  
An issue for which available evidence is insufficient to reach a conclusion.

**Hypothesis**  
A plausible explanation or investigative theory that has not been established as fact.

**Unsupported**  
A claim for which adequate supporting evidence has not been located.

## Treatment of Negative Evidence

The absence of evidence is not treated as evidence of misconduct.

Failure to locate an independent audit, customer testimony, corporate record, or other evidence may be documented as an unresolved diligence question, but is not automatically interpreted as proof that the underlying event did not occur or that misconduct occurred.

## Search and Retrieval Considerations

A large web footprint can result from syndication, SEO, affiliate marketing, public relations, social-media campaigns, translations, and other forms of information distribution.

Such a footprint may affect what information is surfaced by search engines or retrieval systems.

This repository therefore distinguishes between:

1. the existence of information online;
2. the number of apparent publications containing that information;
3. the number of genuinely independent sources supporting the information; and
4. the provenance of the underlying information.

The existence of a large positive web footprint is not, by itself, treated as evidence of independent consensus.

## AI / Search Manipulation Hypothesis

The possibility that coordinated web distribution, SEO, AEO, social-media promotion, or other information-amplification techniques could influence search or AI retrieval systems is treated as an investigative hypothesis unless specific evidence establishes deliberate manipulation of a particular system.

This repository does not use the term "AI poisoning" as an established finding without evidence of activities such as deliberate manipulation of training data, retrieval systems, model inputs, model weights, or a specific model-training pipeline.

## Conflicts of Interest

Potential commercial, financial, professional, or promotional relationships are documented where independently verifiable.

A relationship or conflict of interest does not by itself establish that associated information is false.

It is instead considered when assessing the evidentiary weight and provenance of the material.

## Reproducibility

Where practical, each significant finding should identify:

- The source
- The source type
- The publication or filing date
- The originating source, where applicable
- Whether the material is independent or derivative
- The specific claim supported
- Any relevant limitations
- The date on which the source was accessed

Primary documents should be preferred over secondary descriptions whenever available.

## Corrections

If new evidence contradicts or materially changes a documented finding, the repository should update the finding and explain the change rather than silently removing the previous conclusion.

## Standard of Language

The repository uses factual and qualified language.

It does not characterize a person or organization as fraudulent, deceptive, criminal, manipulative, or otherwise culpable unless the available evidence and applicable primary records support such a characterization.

Where evidence is incomplete, the repository identifies the uncertainty explicitly.
